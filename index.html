01

Content & editorial

A manuscript enters through Submittable and sits in a triage queue — currently a 6–9 month review window. We could help shrink that window and speed things up. Accepted titles go out to freelance editors and proofreaders per-title. Once published, some titles likely need translation for Kube's international Muslim audience — worth confirming — and the backlist builds up metadata and blurbs that need managing, refreshing, and tracking as an ongoing job.

02

Discovery & marketing — what you asked about

A title's discoverability starts at the retailer listing, then depends on the marketing that runs from there — social media, an email list, and paid ads. This is something we can look at immediately, in two ways. First, understanding where things stand today: which channels are generating revenue, and which ones aren't performing as well. Second, customer segmentation across those channels — some customers respond better to email, others to social — moving toward a joined-up, omni-channel view. Both need data analytics to see where you are now before deciding where to focus next.

03

Operations & supply chain

Four back-office functions run behind every sale and every title. Print-run sizing is likely a forecasting decision based on experience and past cycles — worth confirming. Rights and licensing may sit in spreadsheets across imprints and territories. Royalty calculation likely means reconciling statements from multiple retailers by hand. Customer service is handled well by people, which Trustpilot reflects, currently through a phone line restricted to couriers and five separate function-based email inboxes.

Here's how we could help: filling the gaps between the tools you already use, automating manual steps where they exist, replacing spreadsheets with dashboards for a clearer view, and improving traceability across all four.

04

Sales & distribution

Two separate flows run in opposite directions. As distributor, Kube takes on partner publishers' catalogues (Ali Gator, Darussalam, Ta-Ha, IIIT) and sells them outward through its own network into 30–40+ countries, taking a commission. As wholesaler, it runs the reverse: buying bulk stock from mainstream publishers — who typically won't open small accounts directly — and breaking it down into smaller wholesale orders that independent Muslim bookshops can actually place, through kubepublishingtrade.com. Right now that ordering happens by email instead of through the site, and this is somewhere we could help: managing and automating that process, and increasing visibility with dashboards and a unified view — the same kind of CRM approach as the contact-logging piece above.

05

Net Revenue Management — lite

We could use Nielsen BookScan data, where available, to help build a clearer revenue strategy from your own data and where the market's heading — it's a paid, enterprise-priced dataset, so this depends on access. Your existing marketing company has referenced Nielsen — let us know if that access already covers this, so we're not duplicating what you already have.

What's buildable without Nielsen is a directional, not exact, version, built from four pieces below — each from free or already-owned data. We could also help with customer journey mapping, customer behaviour analysis, and omni-channel work, so customers get a consistent experience across every channel.

Channel mix, in plain terms: the breakdown of where sales actually come from — what percentage sells through each channel, rather than treating “sales” as one number. For Kube that'd be something like: X% through kubepublishing.com direct, Y% through Amazon, Z% through the wholesale/trade channel to bookshops, W% through other distributors. It matters because it changes priorities: if most sales run through Amazon, SEO/listing work there matters more than polishing Kube's own site; if wholesale is bigger than it looks, the trade-site fix (section 04) matters even more than the marketing-channel audit does. It's the one NRM-lite piece that's free and already owned — just Kube's own sales records, nothing to scrape or buy.

Price-pack, sharpened: two checks per title — whether it's offered in every format that makes sense for it, and whether the price spread across those formats makes sense (a paperback priced too close to the hardback discourages the higher-margin option instead of protecting it).

06

Financial & strategic — the unified view

Publishing margins are thin industry-wide, so gaps like these tend to compound rather than sit as isolated line items — for example, a slow slush pile delaying revenue, a print-run forecast that ties up cash, or manual royalty reconciliation burning hours that could go to acquisitions. Right now, sales data sits siloed across Amazon, distributors, your own site, Trustpilot, and social. We could help build a single view that helps you understand the root cause of any inefficiencies, or any areas that are draining income or creating cost without a clear return.

Cost & tooling efficiency, worth flagging separately: every business carries a mix of monthly and one-off tool/subscription costs, and the ones not returning real value are easy to lose track of. In a thin-margin business, the highest-leverage move isn't only growing revenue — it's making sure a soft patch in revenue doesn't also mean bleeding cash on subscriptions and tools that aren't earning their keep. A straightforward audit (what's actually being paid for, monthly vs. one-off, what's actually used) usually pays for itself quickly — showing you what's working well versus what isn't, so you can decide where to focus, especially on the areas that aren't.
